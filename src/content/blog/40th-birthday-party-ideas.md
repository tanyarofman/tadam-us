Forty is the birthday people actually want to celebrate: old enough to have stories, young enough to stay up for them. The party works best when it feels like the person turning 40, not like a generic "over the hill" banner. Here are 30 ideas, from a backyard dinner to a weekend away, and how to make the night about their story.

## How do you throw a great 40th birthday party?

Start from the person: who they want there, what they love to eat, and whether they enjoy the spotlight. Then choose one format, one theme and one moment that tells their story.

1. **Ask, or ask around.** Some people want a big party, others want six friends and a long dinner. If it's a surprise, ask their partner or closest friend.
2. **Make the guest list across their life.** Childhood friends, college friends, coworkers and family. A 40th is often the first time all of them are in one room.
3. **Choose the format.** At home, out or away. The table below sorts all 30 ideas.
4. **Send invitations early.** Paperless Post [recommends about four weeks ahead for a casual birthday and six for a formal one](https://www.paperlesspost.com/blog/when-to-send-out-birthday-party-invites).
5. **Plan one personal moment.** A toast, a slideshow or a game about the birthday person. It's what guests remember.

## What are 40th birthday party ideas at home?

At home, choose a format your space can hold: a dinner party for a dozen, a backyard barbecue for fifty, or a game night in the living room. Add one theme and one personal moment, and the party feels planned without a venue.

- **Dinner party (2).** A long table, the birthday person's favorite dishes and one toast per course.
- **Backyard barbecue (1).** Easy for big groups and kids. Rent string lights and extra chairs.
- **Potluck of favorite recipes (7).** Every guest brings a dish that reminds them of the birthday person, with a card explaining why.
- **"40 reasons we love you" (13).** Guests write one reason each on a card before the party. Read some aloud and give all 40 in a box.
- **Trivia night about the birthday person (9).** Questions about their life, answered by the guests. Start from our [100 how well do you know me questions](/blog/how-well-do-you-know-me-questions/), and add a general round from our [100 trivia questions for adults](/blog/general-knowledge-trivia-questions/).
- **Fire pit and s'mores (11).** For a small group that wants to talk late into the night.

## What are 40th birthday ideas for him and for her?

Plan around the person, not around a gender. A food lover gets a tasting or a cooking class, someone who loves the outdoors gets a weekend at a cabin, and a social person gets a big party with everyone they know.

Some starting points by personality:

- **Food and wine:** a dinner party, a tasting or a cooking class (ideas 2, 5 and 18).
- **The outdoors:** a hike and picnic, a lake day or a cabin weekend (25, 27 and 16).
- **Being with everyone they know:** a backyard barbecue, a rented hall or a surprise party (1, 30 and 29).
- **A small circle:** a weekend away or dinner in a private room (15 and 14).
- **Music:** karaoke, a concert or a decade party (8, 22 and 12).
- **Games:** game night, trivia about themselves or an escape room (3, 9 and 19).

## What is a good theme for a 40th birthday?

The best themes come from the birthday person's life: the decade they grew up in, a city they love, or the story of their first 40 years. Generic "over the hill" decor is fine as a joke, but it shouldn't be the whole party.

- **The year they were born.** Someone turning 40 in 2027 was born in 1987. Play the music genres of the late 1980s, serve the snacks they ate as kids and ask guests to dress in the decade.
- **Forty years of their story.** Decorate with photos from every decade of their life, from baby pictures to last summer.
- **Their favorite place.** A Paris dinner, a beach party, or a menu from the city where they met their partner.
- **Aged to perfection.** A wine or whiskey tasting, with a bottle from their birth year if you can find one.
- **Casino night.** Play money, card tables and a prize for the biggest winner. No real betting.

## What games can you play at a 40th birthday party?

Play games about the birthday person: a quiz about their life, a baby photo round or two truths and a lie. They work with 10 guests or 80, and they give guests from different circles something to laugh about together.

- **How well do you know the birthday person.** The classic. Ask 15 to 20 questions, from their first car to their worst haircut.
- **Baby photo match.** Collect baby photos of the guests and guess who's who. The birthday person's photo goes last.
- **Two truths and a lie.** Each guest tells three stories about the birthday person, and the room guesses the lie.
- **Name that tune from their teens.** Two seconds of each song from their high school years.
- **Who said it.** Read out quotes from the birthday person's old texts or yearbook, and guests guess the year.
- **The toast circle.** Each guest shares one sentence about them. Short, warm and often the best part of the night.

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** A 40th at home for 35 guests. The quiz asks what the birthday boy wanted to be at age ten. His college friends say "a pilot". His mother answers "a garbage truck driver, because they got to ride on the back", and the room loses it. His childhood friend wins the quiz by one point and gives the prize, a bottle of wine, to the mom.

## How do you plan a surprise 40th?

Recruit one insider, usually their partner or best friend, to handle the date and the cover story. Invite guests six weeks ahead, tell them to arrive 30 minutes early, and keep everything off social media.

1. **Pick an insider.** They control the birthday person's schedule and get them to the party on time.
2. **Choose a cover story.** A dinner for two, a small gathering at a friend's house, or a fake work event.
3. **Invite with a clear warning.** Put "It's a surprise!" in the first line of the invitation, and use a private group chat that doesn't include the birthday person.
4. **Plan the arrival.** Guests park out of sight and arrive 30 minutes before the birthday person.
5. **Have a plan B.** If the surprise leaks, the party still happens. It just starts with less shouting.
6. **Know the person.** Some people hate surprises. If you're not sure, ask their partner first.

## How do you make the night about the birthday person's story?

Put their story on the screen: photos from every decade, a few short videos from friends who couldn't come, and a game in which every guest answers questions about their life. Keep the toasts short, two or three minutes each.

[Toastmasters suggests keeping a toast to two or three minutes](https://www.toastmasters.org/resources/public-speaking-tips/how-to-give-a-toast), and that goes double when several people speak. Pick three speakers from different parts of the birthday person's life, and let the game do the rest.
