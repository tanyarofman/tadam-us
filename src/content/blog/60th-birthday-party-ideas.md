Sixty is a birthday that brings the whole family together: children, grandchildren, siblings, old friends and the people they worked with for decades. The best 60th parties feel like the person, not like a generic "over the hill" banner. Here are 30 ideas, from a family dinner to a trip, and how to tell the story of their 60 years.

## How do you throw a great 60th birthday party?

Start from the person: who they want there, what they love to eat, and how they feel about the spotlight. Then choose one format, one theme and one moment that tells their story, like a toast, a slideshow or a game.

1. **Ask, or ask around.** Some people want a big party, others want their children and a long lunch. If it's a surprise, ask their partner or closest friend.
2. **Make the guest list across six decades.** Family, childhood friends, old coworkers, neighbors and friends from the clubs and teams they joined.
3. **Choose the format.** At home, out or away. The table below sorts all 30 ideas.
4. **Plan for every age.** Seats for older guests, a quiet corner for conversation, and something for the grandchildren to do.
5. **Plan one personal moment.** A toast, a slideshow or a game about the birthday person. It's what guests remember.

## What are 60th birthday party ideas at home?

At home, choose a format your space can hold: a family dinner for a dozen, a garden party for fifty or a cocktail party in the living room. Add one theme and one personal moment, and the party feels planned without a venue.

- **Family dinner (1).** A long table, their favorite dishes and one toast per course, from a child, a sibling and an old friend.
- **Garden party (3).** Daytime, easy for older guests and grandchildren, with lawn games on the side.
- **Potluck of family recipes (6).** Every guest brings a dish that reminds them of the birthday person, with a card explaining why.
- **"60 reasons we love you" (9).** Guests write one reason each on a card before the party. Read some aloud and give all 60 in a box.
- **Trivia night about them (7).** Questions about their life, answered by the guests. Start from our [100 how well do you know me questions](/blog/how-well-do-you-know-me-questions/) and add a general round from our [100 trivia questions for adults](/blog/general-knowledge-trivia-questions/).
- **A grandchildren's show (30).** The grandchildren sing a song, read a poem or act out a story from their grandparent's life.

## What are 60th birthday ideas for mom, dad, a husband or a wife?

Plan around the person, not around a role. A mom who loves her garden gets a garden party, a dad who loves to golf gets a golf day, and a husband or wife who loves travel gets a trip with the people closest to them.

Some starting points by personality:

- **Food and wine:** a family dinner, a wine tasting or a dinner cruise (ideas 1, 13 and 14).
- **The outdoors:** a garden party, a picnic or a lake house weekend (3, 23 and 21).
- **Being with everyone they know:** a backyard barbecue, a rented hall with a band or a surprise party (2, 28 and 27).
- **Family first:** a family trip, a trip to their hometown or a grandchildren's show (20, 22 and 30).
- **Music and dancing:** a dance party, a concert or a 1967 party (18, 16 and 8).
- **Games:** a casino night, a golf day or trivia about themselves (19, 15 and 7).

For a spouse, the most personal gift is often a memory book (25): letters from friends and family from every decade, collected quietly over a few weeks.

## What is the best theme for a 60th birthday?

The best themes come from their life: the year they were born, the decade they grew up in, or the story of their 60 years. Diamond or silver decor is a classic choice, since a 60th anniversary is called a diamond jubilee.

- **The year they were born.** Someone turning 60 in 2027 was born in 1967, the year of the [first Super Bowl](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Super-Bowl-I) and the [Summer of Love](https://www.britannica.com/event/Summer-of-Love-1967) in San Francisco. Play the music of the late 1960s and serve the party food of the era.
- **Diamond or silver.** A [diamond jubilee is a 60th anniversary](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/diamond-jubilee), so white, silver and sparkle all fit (idea 29).
- **Sixty years of their story.** Decorate with photos from every decade, from baby pictures to last summer (idea 10).
- **Their favorite place.** A beach party, an Italian dinner or a menu from the town where they grew up.
- **The decade they were young.** A 1980s dance party, if that's when they had the most fun.

## How do you throw a 60th birthday party on a budget?

Host at home or in a park, let guests bring the food, and spend on one thing the birthday person will treasure. A potluck, a picnic or a bonfire costs little, and a memory book or video messages cost almost nothing.

- **Potluck of family recipes (6).** The guests bring the food, you bring the cake.
- **Picnic in a park (23).** Many city parks let you reserve a pavilion. Check the rules for groups first.
- **Bonfire night (24).** A backyard, a fire and old stories told late into the night.
- **Video messages (26).** Ask friends and relatives who can't come to record 30 seconds each, and play them at the party.
- **Memory book (25).** A shared online folder of letters and photos, printed as a book.

## What games can you play at a 60th birthday party?

Play games about the birthday person: a quiz about their life, a guess-the-decade photo round or two truths and a lie. They work for every age, from grandchildren to old friends, and give guests who've never met something to laugh about together.

- **How well do you know the birthday person.** Fifteen to twenty questions, from their first car to the year they met their partner.
- **Guess the decade.** Show photos of the birthday person, and guests guess the decade each one is from.
- **Two truths and a lie.** Each guest tells three stories about the birthday person, and the room guesses the lie.
- **Name that tune from 1967.** Two seconds of each song from the year they were born.
- **Who said it.** Read out quotes from the birthday person over the years, and guests guess the decade.
- **Grandchildren versus grownups.** The same quiz, played by two teams.

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** A 60th for 50 guests in a restaurant's back room. The quiz asks what the birthday mom did on her first day of work in 1985. Her coworkers guess "got lost in the building". Her husband says "spilled coffee on the boss". Her best friend from high school knows the real answer: she locked herself out of the car with the engine running. The grandchildren's team still wins.

## How do you make the night about their 60 years?

Put their six decades on the screen: photos from every one of them, short videos from friends far away, and a game in which every guest answers questions about their life. Keep the toasts short, two or three minutes each.

[Toastmasters suggests keeping a toast to two or three minutes](https://www.toastmasters.org/resources/public-speaking-tips/how-to-give-a-toast), and that matters more when several people speak. Pick three speakers from different decades of their life, and let the game do the rest.
