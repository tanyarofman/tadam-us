Baby shower games have a reputation: diapers, melted chocolate and a lot of polite laughing. They don't have to be like that. The games guests remember are quick, a little funny and about the people having the baby. Here are 30 games, from the classics to the ones that work at a coed shower, and how many to actually play.

## What are the most popular baby shower games?

The most popular baby shower games are baby photo match, guess the baby food, baby shower bingo, a which-parent quiz and the price is right with baby items. They're easy to set up, work for any size of group and need only printed cards or a few props.

A few notes:

- **Which parent (1).** Every guest gets a paddle with a photo of each parent-to-be on either side. The host reads "who will cry first at preschool drop-off" and everyone raises a side.
- **Don't say baby (10).** Each guest gets a clothespin. Say "baby" and anyone who hears it takes your pin. Most pins at the end wins.
- **Guess the due date (7) and weight (24).** Collect ballots at the shower and announce the winner after the birth.
- **Baby name meanings (17).** Read out the meaning of a name, and guests guess the name. End with the names the parents are considering, if they're sharing.

## Which baby shower games aren't cheesy?

The games that don't feel cheesy are about the parents-to-be, not about diapers: a which-parent quiz, a who-said-it round with the parents' own quotes, or trivia about their childhoods. Guests laugh because they learn something, not because they're asked to.

- **Which parent (1).** Short, funny and everyone plays.
- **Who said it (12).** Quotes from each parent-to-be about the baby, the birth plan or their own childhood. Guests guess who said what.
- **Baby trivia (16).** Questions about the parents as babies: their first word, their worst habit, the nickname their parents used.
- **Advice cards (22).** Guests write advice for the first year, and the parents read a few aloud.
- **Onesie decorating (21).** A quiet craft that leaves the parents with a drawer of one-of-a-kind onesies.

Skip anything that asks guests to taste mystery food from a diaper, unless you know your crowd loves it.

## What is the funniest baby shower game?

Which parent and who said it are usually the funniest, because the answers are about real people in the room. Guess the baby food and the toilet paper game get big laughs too, but they're funnier with a crowd that knows each other well.

For the which-parent questions, ask both parents-to-be beforehand and use the ones where they disagree:

- Who will change the most diapers?
- Who will be the stricter parent?
- Who will cry first at the first day of school?
- Who will be the one up at 3 a.m.?
- Who will take a thousand photos a day?
- Who will spoil the baby more?
- Who will teach the baby to drive?
- Who will be better at bath time?

## What games work at a coed baby shower?

At a coed shower, choose games about the couple and team games guests can play from their seats: which parent, a couple trivia round, baby photo match and a nursery rhyme quiz. Avoid games that center on only one parent-to-be.

- **Which parent (1).** Built for a coed shower: both parents are in the spotlight.
- **Mommy or daddy quiz (26).** Guests guess which parent a childhood story belongs to.
- **Baby bottle race (25).** Volunteers race to finish a baby bottle of their favorite drink. Loud and short.
- **Diaper the baby (18).** Partners race to diaper a doll, one hand each.
- **Who is more likely (29).** The couple answers with paddles, the room votes too.

## How many games should you play at a baby shower?

Two or three games are enough for most baby showers, plus one ongoing activity like a diaper raffle or advice cards. A shower usually lasts two to three hours, and guests also need time to eat, talk and watch the gifts.

A simple plan for two and a half hours:

1. **First 30 minutes:** arrivals, food and an ongoing game (don't say baby, guess the due date).
2. **Next 20 minutes:** the main game, with everyone in the room.
3. **Next 45 minutes:** gifts, with bingo for the guests who are watching.
4. **Next 15 minutes:** a short second game.
5. **Last 40 minutes:** cake, the raffle winner and goodbyes.

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** A coed baby shower for 30 guests. In the which-parent game, the question "Who will be up at 3 a.m." gets nearly every paddle on the dad-to-be. The mom-to-be raises his side too. He raises hers, because "I sleep through fire alarms", and his own mother confirms it from the back of the room.

## What can you do at a baby shower besides play games?

Plan activities guests can join at their own pace: onesie decorating, advice cards, wishes for the baby, a diaper raffle or a book for the baby's library. They keep the room busy without asking anyone to perform.

- **Onesie decorating (21).** Fabric markers and plain onesies.
- **Wishes for baby (23).** Cards to open on the baby's first birthday.
- **A book for the baby's library.** Guests bring a favorite children's book with a note inside.
- **Diaper raffle (11).** Every pack of diapers a guest brings is a raffle ticket.
- **A memory station.** Photos of both parents as babies, side by side.

For invitations, the Emily Post Institute suggests [sending them three to six weeks before the shower](https://emilypost.com/advice/baby-showers-party-and-invitation-timing), so guests have time to RSVP and choose a gift.

## What are good prizes for baby shower games?

Good prizes are small, useful and the same for every game: a candle, a coffee gift card, a plant, nice chocolate or a bottle of wine. Let the winner choose from a basket, so the prizes go to people who want them.

- **A prize basket.** Five to eight small prizes, and winners choose in order.
- **Something useful.** A candle, a hand cream, a nice pen or a gift card for coffee.
- **Something edible.** Cookies, chocolate or a jar of local honey.
- **Something for the table.** The winner of the last game takes home the centerpiece.
