Every parent planning a bar mitzvah reaches the same question at some point: what will there be at the event besides food and dancing? The 25 bar mitzvah ideas below, the entertainment Israelis call attractions (אטרקציות), are organized by type, with a short note on who each attraction suits, a table of what works in a hall and what works at home, and how to choose without ending up with five attractions that all do the same thing.

## How do you choose a bar mitzvah attraction?

Start from the crowd and from the moment your event is missing, not from the vendor's catalog. There are usually plenty of photo stations, and what's missing is one moment when the whole hall does something together.

1. **Who's the crowd?** An event with 80 friends from class and 20 adults needs something different from a family event with three generations.
2. **What does your son or daughter want?** Some kids dream of a wild dance floor, and some would rather not be put in the middle.
3. **Which moment is missing?** The reception, the dance floor, or a peak moment in the middle of the evening.

## Which attractions suit a bar mitzvah in a hall, and which suit one at home?

A hall has room for a dance floor, a stage and large stations. At home or in the garden, small attractions that need no setup work better, along with activities everyone can do from their seat.

| Attraction | In a hall | At home or in the garden | Especially good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magnet photo station | Yes | Yes, if there's a free corner | All ages |
| 360 video booth | Yes | Only in a large garden | Teens |
| Drum show | Yes | No, too loud | Entrance, dance floor |
| Candy or shake station | Yes | Yes | Kids and teens |
| Magician or mentalist | Yes | Yes | All ages |
| Mobile escape room | Less so | Yes | Friends from class |
| Trivia game about the bar mitzvah boy | Yes, on the projector | Yes, on the TV | All ages together |

## Which photo and keepsake attractions suit a bar mitzvah?

Photo stations are the most common attraction, because guests take a keepsake home. Pick one, not three.

1. **Magnet photo station.** The classic, and it works for every age.
2. **360 video booth.** A rotating platform that films a short clip. Kids and teens love it.
3. **Interactive selfie mirror.** A large mirror with a touch screen, printing and animations.
4. **AI photo station.** You get photographed and receive a picture in a cartoon style or in another world.
5. **Digital guest book.** Guests record a short video greeting, and you get all of them at the end of the evening.
6. **Paparazzi photographer.** A photographer who moves between the tables and shoots the guests as if they were on a red carpet.

## Which attractions bring energy to the dance floor?

Drums, LED accessories and dancers pull people onto the floor in an instant. They work best at the bar mitzvah boy's entrance and in the second round of dancing.

7. **Drum show.** Brings energy to the dance floor in a single moment.
8. **Cold sparklers.** For the entrance or for the slow dance. Check with the hall in advance whether they're allowed.
9. **LED and glow accessories.** Glasses, bracelets and hats handed out in the middle of the evening.
10. **UV party.** Purple lighting, glowing colors and a party atmosphere. Especially loved at bat mitzvahs.
11. **Professional dancers.** They draw the guests onto the floor and teach a short choreography.
12. **Smoke or confetti machine.** For the peak moment, not for the whole evening.

## Which food stations are popular at a bar mitzvah?

Sweet stations and shakes are the kids' favorite, and a food truck suits an outdoor event. They don't replace the meal, they give guests a reason to wander around.

13. **Shake or mocktail station.** For the teens, with drinks named after the bar mitzvah boy.
14. **Candy or cotton candy station.** Simple, and there's never anything left of it.
15. **Food truck.** Burgers, fries or ice cream, for outdoor events.
16. **A chef who cooks in front of the guests.** Suits a small, intimate event.

## Which shows suit a bar mitzvah?

A short show, up to 15 minutes, works better than a long one that competes with the meal. Choose a show that makes both the teens and the adults laugh.

17. **Magician or mentalist.** Moves between the tables or takes the stage for ten minutes.
18. **Stand-up comedian.** Makes the adults laugh, and sometimes embarrasses the teens. Choose carefully.
19. **Live band.** For the reception or for family songs.
20. **Fire or acrobatics show.** For outdoor events with plenty of space.

## Which attractions involve all the guests, from Grandma to the friends from class?

These are the attractions people are still talking about a week later. What they have in common: they're about the bar or bat mitzvah kid, and all the guests take part instead of just watching.

21. **A live trivia game about the bar mitzvah boy.** Questions and stories from family and friends, on the big screen. Everyone scans a code and answers from their phone, and Grandma competes with the friends from class over who knows him best. This is what we do at [Tadam!](/).
22. **Slideshow or video clip.** The classic way to tell your child's story. Keep it short, up to 5 minutes.
23. **Mobile escape room.** A group game for the friends from class, good for a bar mitzvah in a small hall or at home.
24. **Family karaoke.** Each generation picks one song.
25. **"Who knows me best" on stage.** A simple version of a quiz, with a few representatives who come up on stage. It works, but only the people on stage really take part.

## Why does a trivia game about the bar mitzvah boy work so well?

Because it's the one moment of the evening when the whole hall stops and does something together, and every question is a memory of someone who is in the room. Grandma beats the friends from class, and everyone laughs.

2. **You pick a host and review the questions.** We draft them from your stories, and you can edit or delete any of them, so nobody gets embarrassed.
3. **You get the game** as a private link by email, with a host who runs it.
4. **At the event**, the DJ stops the music for about 15 minutes. Everyone scans, plays, and at the end we announce who knows the bar mitzvah boy best.

No app and no stage needed, and older guests who aren't comfortable with phones play together with the grandchildren.
