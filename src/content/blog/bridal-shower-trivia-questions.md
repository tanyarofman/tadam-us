Every shower has a few people who are sure they know the bride better than anyone: the childhood friend, the college roommate, her mom. Bridal shower trivia settles it. Here are 75 questions about the bride, the couple and the funny side of both, and how to run the quiz so everyone in the room plays.

## How does "who knows the bride best" work?

The host reads questions about the bride, every guest answers on a card or a phone, and the bride reveals the right answer. Each right answer is a point, and the guest with the most points knows the bride best.

You need three things:

1. **15 to 25 questions** from the lists below, with the bride's answers.
2. **A host** who reads the questions and keeps the pace.
3. **A way to answer and score.** Cards, slides on a TV or a live game on phones. The table compares them.

| | Paper cards | Slides on a TV | Live game on phones |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Setup time** | About an hour: print cards, write the answer key | Two to three hours: build slides with photos | Depends on the tool: send the questions and answers |
| **Scoring** | Swap cards and mark, then add up | The host counts answers by hand | Automatic, with live scores |
| **Remote guests** | Only if you mail or email cards ahead | Yes, by sharing the screen on a video call | Ask the tool you use before the shower |

Run it after the meal, while guests are still seated. If you have more games planned, our list of [25 bridal shower games guests actually enjoy](/blog/bridal-shower-games/) helps you choose what goes before and after.

## How do you collect the answers without spoiling the surprise?

Ask the bride for her answers privately, a week before the shower, and don't tell her which questions you'll use. For the couple and funny questions, ask her partner or a parent instead.

A few ways that keep the game a surprise:

- **Send her a long list.** Ask 40 questions and use 20. She won't know which ones made the cut.
- **Ask her partner.** For couple questions, the partner's version is often funnier, and the bride can confirm or argue at the shower.
- **Ask the family for the old stories.** A parent or sibling knows the childhood nickname and the worst haircut.
- **Record the answers on video.** The partner answers on camera, and you play each clip as the reveal.
- **Keep one answer key.** Only the host holds it. Don't put the answers in the group chat.
