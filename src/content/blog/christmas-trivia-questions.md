Christmas trivia works at any December gathering: a family dinner, a party with friends or an office lunch. These 60 questions cover the history of the holiday, traditions, carols, food and customs around the world, with a hard round for the know-it-alls and easy questions for kids. Every answer was checked against a reliable source, and the questions stay right year after year. (In the UK, the same game is called a Christmas quiz.)

## What are good Christmas trivia questions?

Good Christmas trivia questions have one clear answer, teach the room something and mix easy with hard. Plan rounds of about 8 to 10 questions on different topics, so every guest has a round they're good at.

The rounds below: history (1 to 10), traditions (11 to 20), carols (21 to 28), food (29 to 36), around the world (37 to 44), a hard round (45 to 52) and kids (53 to 60). For an hour of play, choose three or four rounds. If you need more general questions, add a few from our [100 trivia questions for adults](/blog/general-knowledge-trivia-questions/).

## What are some Christmas history trivia questions?

History questions cover where Christmas customs came from: the Roman festival of Saturnalia, the first Christmas card in 1843 and the year Christmas became a US federal holiday. They're the best round for adults who like a story behind the answer.

The answers come from [Britannica on why Christmas is in December](https://www.britannica.com/story/why-is-christmas-in-december) and [how the Christmas tree tradition started](https://www.britannica.com/story/how-did-the-tradition-of-christmas-trees-start), [Time on the federal holiday](https://time.com/4608452/christmas-america-national-holiday/), the [Library of Congress on the first Christmas card](https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2019/12/dear-whoever-you-are-heres-the-first-christmas-card/), [History.com on the Massachusetts ban](https://www.history.com/articles/when-massachusetts-banned-christmas), the [National Park Service on the first National Christmas Tree](https://www.nps.gov/whho/learn/historyculture/1923-national-christmas-tree.htm) and Merriam-Webster on [Noel](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/noel) and [Xmas](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/Xmas).

1. Which Roman festival in December is often linked to Christmas customs? **Saturnalia**
2. In which year did Christmas become a US federal holiday? **1870**
3. In which country was the first commercial Christmas card printed, in 1843? **England**
4. Which American colony banned celebrating Christmas from 1659 to 1681? **The Massachusetts Bay Colony**
5. In which year was the first National Christmas Tree lit in Washington, DC? **1923**
6. In which year was Charles Dickens's "A Christmas Carol" published? **1843**
7. The word "Noel" comes from a Latin word meaning what? **Birth**
8. What does the X in "Xmas" stand for? **The Greek letter chi, the first letter of "Christ" in Greek**
9. In which country did the decorated Christmas tree tradition begin? **Germany**
10. A newspaper picture of which British royal family around a Christmas tree made trees fashionable in 1848? **Queen Victoria's**

## What are hard Christmas trivia questions?

Hard Christmas questions ask for exact numbers, years and names: the total gifts in "The Twelve Days of Christmas", the year "Silent Night" was first sung, or the man the poinsettia is named after. Save them for a final round or a tiebreaker.

Many have number answers, so give a point for the closest guess if nobody gets it exactly. The total of 364 gifts is simple math: add every gift in all 12 verses, as [Princeton's Cotsen Children's Library](https://blogs.princeton.edu/cotsen/tag/james-pitt) shows. The poem in question 51 is covered by [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/A-Visit-from-St-Nicholas).

45. How many gifts are given in total across all 12 verses of "The Twelve Days of Christmas"? **364**
46. Who commissioned the first commercial Christmas card in 1843? **Henry Cole**
47. In which year was "Silent Night" first performed? **1818**
48. In which year was "Jingle Bells" first published? **1857**
49. After which US diplomat, the first US minister to Mexico, is the poinsettia named? **Joel Roberts Poinsett**
50. In which year was "O Holy Night" first performed? **1847**
51. In which year was the poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas" first published? **1823**
52. Which US president signed the law that made Christmas a federal holiday? **Ulysses S. Grant**

## What are Christmas trivia questions for kids?

Kids' Christmas trivia should be short and a little surprising: how many sides a snowflake has, what reindeer eat and whether girl reindeer grow antlers. Let kids answer out loud and give a point for every brave guess.

The answers come from [NOAA on snowflakes](https://www.noaa.gov/stories/how-do-snowflakes-form-science-behind-snow), the [National Park Service on lichen and caribou](https://www.nps.gov/articles/lichensandcaribou.htm), the [Alaska Department of Fish and Game on caribou](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=viewing.landmammals&species=caribou), [History.com on milk and cookies for Santa](https://www.history.com/articles/dont-forget-santas-cookies-and-milk-the-history-of-a-popular-christmas-tradition), [National Geographic on candy canes](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/the-sweet-and-sticky-story-of-candy-canes) and [Britannica on Christmas](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Christmas).

53. How many sides does a snowflake have? **Six**
54. What do reindeer eat in winter? **Lichen, also called reindeer moss**
55. What do kids in the US leave out for Santa? **Milk and cookies**
56. True or false: girl reindeer grow antlers too. **True**
57. How many reindeer pull the sleigh in the poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas"? **Eight**
58. What color were the very first candy canes? **All white**
59. In Australia, is Christmas in summer or winter? **Summer**
60. True or false: "Jingle Bells" was first written as a Christmas song. **False**

## How do you host a Christmas trivia game?

Split guests into mixed teams of three to six, read each question twice and give teams 30 seconds to write their answer. Score one point per answer, read the answers after each round, and keep a hard question ready as a tiebreaker.

- **Teams:** mix ages and families, so no team is all experts.
- **Answer sheets:** one per team per round, with a team name on top.
- **Timing:** 30 seconds per question, about 10 minutes per round.
- **Fun facts:** after each answer, add one line of the story behind it.
- **Tiebreaker:** a number question, like the total gifts in "The Twelve Days of Christmas". Closest answer wins.
- **Prizes:** first pick of dessert, a paper crown or choosing the next activity. For more ideas between rounds, see our [25 Christmas games for family](/blog/christmas-games-for-family/).
