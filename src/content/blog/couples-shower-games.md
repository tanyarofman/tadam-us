A couples shower, also called a Jack and Jill shower, celebrates both partners with guests from both sides. The games are different from a bridal shower: both partners play, the questions are about the two of them, and everyone, not only the bride's friends, gets a turn. Here are 25 games and ideas, and how to plan the afternoon around them.

## What is a couples shower?

A couples shower is a wedding shower for both partners, with a guest list of friends and family from both sides. It has the gifts and the games of a bridal shower, but the groom (or the second partner) is there and in the spotlight too.

[Emily Post notes that couples' showers have become common](https://emilypost.com/advice/shower-etiquette) as a modern take on the traditional bride-only party. Zola draws a useful line: a [Jack and Jill shower celebrates both partners](https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/coed-wedding-shower), while a coed bridal shower has a mixed guest list but still centers on the bride. This article is about the first kind.

Some regions use "Jack and Jill" for a ticketed fundraiser party before the wedding. If you're writing the invitation, "couples shower" avoids the confusion.

## What are good couples shower ideas and themes?

Choose a theme from the couple's life: how they met, a place they love, or something they do together. A good theme sets the food, the decor and the games, so you don't need to plan each one separately.

- **Their love story.** Photos from the first date to the proposal, and a trivia round about the timeline.
- **Stock the bar.** Guests bring a bottle or a bar tool, and the couple leaves with a full bar cart.
- **Backyard barbecue.** Easy for a big mixed crowd, with lawn games on the side.
- **Brunch.** Mimosas, waffles and a short game before the gifts.
- **Their favorite city.** A menu and playlist from the city where they met or got engaged.
- **Game night.** Board games, cards and one big trivia round about the couple.
- **Wine and cheese tasting.** Guests bring a bottle from a region the couple loves.
- **Around the clock.** Each guest is given an hour of the day and brings a gift for it, like coffee mugs for 8 a.m.

For a couple that has everything, a "stock the bar", "date night" or "around the clock" shower gives guests a clear idea and the couple gifts they'll actually use.

A few notes on the favorites:

- **What did they say (1).** Before the shower, ask each partner 10 questions about the other. At the shower, read the question, let the other partner guess, then reveal the real answer.
- **Shoe game (2).** The couple sits back to back, each holding one of their own shoes and one of their partner's, and raises a shoe to answer "who is more likely to…". See our [shoe game questions](/blog/wedding-shoe-game-questions/).
- **Couple trivia (3).** Questions about the couple, answered by guests in teams. The best friend on one side usually loses to a sibling on the other.
- **Wedding guessing game (14).** Guests guess the first dance song, how long the speeches will run and who cries first. Open the ballots after the wedding.

## Is there a shower game where the groom answers questions?

Yes: it's usually called "what did he say" or "what did the groom say", and at a couples shower both partners play it. Each answers the same questions about the other beforehand, and the room hears both answers at the shower.

Ask each partner the questions separately, a week or so before, and record or write down their answers. At the shower, read the question aloud, ask the other partner to guess what their partner said, and then reveal it. Score one point per match, and keep a tally on a board.

Good questions:

- Where was your first date, and who paid?
- What's the one chore your partner always skips?
- What did you think the first time you met?
- Who said "I love you" first?
- What's your partner's go-to order at your favorite restaurant?
- Who's the better driver?

## How many games should you play at a couples shower?

Two or three games are enough for most couples showers, plus one ongoing activity like advice cards. A shower usually lasts about three hours, and guests also need time to eat, talk and watch the gifts.

A planner quoted by The Knot calls [three hours or less the typical sweet spot for a shower](https://www.theknot.com/content/how-long-are-bridal-showers). A simple plan for three hours:

1. **First 45 minutes:** arrivals, drinks, and an ongoing activity, like advice cards or a date night jar.
2. **Next 30 minutes:** food.
3. **Next 30 minutes:** the main game, with everyone in the room.
4. **Next 45 minutes:** gifts, with bingo for the guests who are watching.
5. **Last 30 minutes:** a short second game, toasts and goodbyes.

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** A couples shower for 40 guests in a backyard. The main game asks both partners where they'd go if they could move anywhere. She answers "Lisbon". He answers "her parents' basement, rent free". Her friends and his friends laugh at the same moment for the first time all afternoon, and from then on the two sides mix.

## What can you do instead of games at a couples shower?

If the couple doesn't like games, plan activities guests can join at their own pace: advice cards, a date night jar, a recipe swap or a memory jar. A short toast from one friend on each side also gives the shower a center without any games.

- **Advice cards (8).** Guests write one piece of marriage advice, and the couple reads them on their first anniversary.
- **Date night jar (9).** Each guest writes one date idea and drops it in a jar.
- **Recipe swap (11).** Each guest brings a favorite recipe on a card for the couple's first kitchen.
- **Memory jar (24).** Guests write a memory of the couple, and a few are read aloud.
- **Toasts.** One friend from each side, two minutes each.
