## Is Friendsgiving a thing?

Yes. Friendsgiving is a Thanksgiving-style meal shared with friends instead of family, usually a potluck, and MWFACT

Friendsgiving has no fixed date. Many groups meet on a weekend in November, before or after Thanksgiving, so nobody has to choose between friends and family. The host usually makes the turkey or the main dish, and everyone else brings a side, a dessert or drinks. Because there's no family tradition to follow, the group gets to invent its own, and games are the easiest one to start.

## What are funny Friendsgiving game ideas?

The funniest Friendsgiving games are about the friends: guess who made each potluck dish, vote on superlatives like most likely to burn the rolls, and read anonymous answers out loud for "who said it". Keep the jokes affectionate, so everyone laughs, including the person in the spotlight.

**1. Guess who brought it.** Number every dish on the table without names. Everyone writes down who they think made each one, then the cooks reveal themselves. Points for every right guess.

**2. Potluck awards.** Before dessert, everyone votes in categories: best side, most creative dish, best comeback for store-bought, the dish everyone went back for. Hand out handwritten certificates.

**3. Friend superlatives.** Write categories on paper, like "most likely to forget the group chat" or "best road trip partner", and vote by secret ballot.

**4. Who said it.** Ask everyone a few questions ahead of time, like their worst haircut or first concert. Read the answers out loud and guess who said each one.

**13. Most likely to.** Someone reads a prompt, like "most likely to move abroad", and on three everyone points at a friend. The one with the most fingers pointed at them has to explain.

## What are Friendsgiving games for work?

At an office Friendsgiving, keep games short and safe for everyone: guess who brought each dish, a gratitude round, two truths and a lie, and a fishbowl game in mixed teams. Skip anything too personal, and let people pass.

- **Guess who brought it (1)** works especially well at work, because it gets people talking about recipes and family traditions.
- **Two truths and a lie (5):** everyone shares three facts and the table guesses the lie.
- **Fishbowl (17):** everyone writes three words on slips. Teams play three rounds with the same slips: describe the word, then one word only, then act it out.
- **Gratitude toast (6):** each person thanks a coworker for one thing from this year.

## What games can you play at a Friendsgiving dinner table?

Play games that need no moving and no supplies: the gratitude ABC, a question jar, two truths and a lie and most likely to. Everyone can keep eating while they play.

- **Gratitude ABC (14).** Go around the table, and each person names something they're thankful for that starts with the next letter of the alphabet. Whoever gets stuck on X has to think fast.
- **Question jar (19).** Fill a jar with questions like "best meal you ever had" or "a skill you wish you had". Pass it around and everyone draws one.
- **Hot seat (12).** One friend sits in the hot seat and answers three quick questions from the table, no skipping.
- **Gratitude toast (6).** Before the first bite, everyone raises a glass and says one sentence about why they're thankful for the person to their left.
- **Wishbone pull (16).** Save the turkey's wishbone, dry it, and let two friends pull it at the end of the night. The longer piece gets the wish.

## What should you bring to a Friendsgiving?

Bring a dish to share in a container you don't need back, and ask the host what's missing before you choose. Drinks, ice, a dessert or a game for after dinner are always welcome.

- **A dish:** check with the host first, so the table doesn't end up with five mac and cheeses. Label it if it has nuts, meat or gluten.
- **Serving gear:** a spoon to serve it and anything it needs to stay warm.
- **Drinks or ice:** easy to bring and always needed.
- **Containers:** for the leftover draft at the end of the night (15), where friends take turns picking leftovers to take home.
- **A game:** bingo cards, a question jar or ten trivia questions about the group.
