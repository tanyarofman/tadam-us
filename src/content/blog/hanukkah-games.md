The candles burn for half an hour, the latkes disappear in ten minutes, and the night is still young. A good game is what keeps everyone at the table after the menorah is lit. Here's how to play dreidel properly, plus 12 more Hanukkah games for every age.

## When is Hanukkah 2026?

Hanukkah 2026 begins at sundown on Friday, December 4, and ends at nightfall on Saturday, December 12. That gives you eight nights of candles, gelt and games.

According to [Hebcal's Hanukkah 2026 calendar](https://www.hebcal.com/holidays/chanukah-2026), the first candle is lit on Friday evening, December 4. Because the first night falls on a weekend, it's a natural night for a family party with games. You don't need a new game every night: pick two or three and repeat the favorites.

## How do you play the dreidel game?

Everyone gets the same amount of gelt and puts one piece in the pot. Players take turns spinning the dreidel, and the letter facing up decides whether you take from the pot, give to it, or do nothing.

1. **Hand out the gelt.** Give each player 10 to 15 pieces: chocolate coins, pennies, raisins or nuts.
2. **Fill the pot.** Everyone puts one piece in the middle. Whenever the pot is empty (or down to one piece), everyone adds one again.
3. **Spin in turn.** Spin the dreidel by its handle on a flat table. When it falls, read the letter on top.
4. **Follow the letter.** Nun: nothing. Gimel: take the whole pot. Hei: take half (round up if it's odd). Shin: put one in.
5. **Pass it on.** The dreidel goes to the next player, clockwise.

Young kids can play with a grown-up as a partner who reads the letters. If you have more than eight players, split into two tables with a dreidel each.

## What do the letters on a dreidel mean?

The four Hebrew letters stand for "Nes gadol haya sham," meaning "a great miracle happened there." In the game, each letter also stands for a Yiddish word that tells you what to do.

| Letter | Hebrew | Yiddish | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nun | נ | Nisht (nothing) | Nothing |
| Gimel | ג | Gantz (all) | Take the pot |
| Hei | ה | Halb (half) | Take half |
| Shin | ש | Shtel (put in) | Add one |

Some dreidels have a pe instead of a shin. If yours does, play it the same way as shin. The [Wikipedia entry on the dreidel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreidel) lists the Yiddish words behind each letter.

## How does the dreidel game end, and how do you win?

The traditional game ends when one player has all the gelt. At a family party, it's easier to set a timer for 15 or 20 minutes and let whoever has the most gelt win.

There's no skill in where the dreidel lands, so anyone can win, from a four-year-old to Grandpa. Players who run out of gelt are out, or you can let them borrow one piece from the bank to stay in. The one trick that helps: a good, long spin, so the dreidel doesn't fall over before it gets going.

## Is the dreidel game gambling?

It started as a gambling game. Today families play it for chocolate coins, and the letters carry the Hanukkah message.

The [Library of Congress Folklife Today blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2022/12/the-truth-behind-the-hanukkah-dreidel-metafolklore-play-and-spin/) traces the dreidel to the teetotum, a spinning top used in betting games in England and Ireland. Jewish families in German-speaking lands played their own version with Hebrew letters, and the explanation linking the letters to the miracle came later. Playing for chocolate keeps the fun and leaves out the betting.

## What other Hanukkah games can families play?

The best Hanukkah games are short, need little or no equipment, and let kids and adults play on the same team. Here are 12 that work alongside dreidel.

1. **Dreidel tournament.** Everyone gets their own dreidel and competes in heats (details below).
2. **Hanukkah bingo.** Five-by-five cards filled with Hanukkah words.
3. **Gelt hunt.** Chocolate coins hidden around the house, with clues.
4. **Latke taste test.** Blindfolded guessing of toppings.
5. **Hanukkah charades.** Act out holiday words with no talking.
6. **Eyes-closed menorah drawing.** Draw a menorah with nine candles without looking.
7. **Who am I?** A sticky note on your forehead and yes-or-no questions.
8. **Hum that song.** Hum a holiday song, and the others guess it.
9. **Jelly doughnut decorating.** Plain doughnuts, toppings and a five-minute timer.
10. **Eight nights, eight challenges.** One small challenge for each night of Hanukkah.
11. **Hanukkah pass the parcel.** Gelt in the middle, a challenge in every layer.
12. **Family Hanukkah trivia.** Questions about the holiday and about your own family.

For Hanukkah bingo, fill the squares from this word list, in a different order on every card: dreidel, menorah, shamash, latke, jelly doughnut, gelt, Maccabees, Judah, Temple, oil, miracle, Kislev, eight, candle, wick, light, applesauce, sour cream, blessing, gimel, nun, hei, shin, and one free space.

## Which Hanukkah games work for kids?

Kids love games with movement, searching and sticky fingers. These work from about age four, at home or at a class party.

**Gelt hunt.** Hide chocolate coins around the house, with a clue at each spot that leads to the next one. For kids who can't read yet, draw the clues.

**Jelly doughnut decorating.** Set out plain doughnuts, frosting, sprinkles and chocolate chips, and give everyone five minutes. A grandparent is the judge.

**Eyes-closed menorah drawing.** Everyone draws a menorah with nine candles, eyes shut. Hang the drawings up and vote for the best and the funniest.

**Eight nights, eight challenges.** One small challenge per night: sing the blessing in a robot voice, tell a joke, say something nice about the person on your left.

**Dreidel tournament.** Give each kid a dreidel and run short heats: the longest spin, an upside-down spin on the handle, a spin with the other hand, and a spin that has to stay on a plate. Different heats mean different winners.

## Which Hanukkah games work for adults?

Adults enjoy guessing games, memory games and a bit of friendly competition. These work after dinner, at a party with friends, or with the whole extended family.

**Hanukkah charades.** Act it out with no words: "frying latkes," "the oil that lasted eight days," "searching for the shamash in the box."

**Who am I?** Stick a note on everyone's forehead with a holiday word or a family member's name. Only yes-or-no questions are allowed.

**Latke taste test.** Set out latkes with different toppings (applesauce, sour cream, smoked salmon, something surprising) and guess blindfolded.

**Whose Hanukkah memory is it?** Everyone writes down a Hanukkah memory from childhood. Read them out without names and guess whose each one is.

**Hanukkah pass the parcel.** Wrap gelt in many layers with a challenge in each one. Our [pass the parcel tasks and questions](/blog/pass-the-parcel-tasks/) give you plenty to choose from.

## Which Hanukkah games work for a big group?

Choose games where everyone plays at once, not one person at a time. Bingo and team trivia work with 30 guests or more.

**Hanukkah bingo.** Print one card per guest and have a host call the words. The first full row, column or diagonal wins a bag of gelt.

**Family Hanukkah trivia.** Split into mixed-age teams and mix questions about the holiday with questions about the family.
