## How do you play dreidel, and what are the rules?

Every player puts one coin in the pot, and spins the dreidel in turn. The letter the dreidel lands on decides what happens: you take nothing, take everything, take half, or add a coin.

1. **Hand out coins.** Ten to fifteen per player: chocolate coins, nuts or almonds.
2. **Everyone puts a coin in the pot.** When the pot is empty, everyone puts in again.
3. **Spin in turn.** Nun: nothing happens. Gimel: you take the whole pot. Hei: you take half the pot. Pe (or shin on a dreidel from outside Israel): you add a coin to the pot.
4. **Whoever runs out of coins is out.** The winner is the last one left, or whoever has the most when time is up.

[These rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreidel) come from Yiddish: nisht (nothing), gantz (everything), halb (half) and shtel arayn (put in). The dreidel itself is a Jewish version of a European gambling top, and the reading "a great miracle happened there" was attached to the letters later.

## How do you run a dreidel tournament?

Give every player a dreidel, and compete in a few short heats. Each heat tests a different skill, so it's not always the same kid who wins.

- **The long spin.** Everyone spins together, and the last dreidel to fall wins.
- **Upside-down spin.** Spin the dreidel on its handle.
- **Other hand.** Right-handers spin with their left, and left-handers with their right.
- **Target spin.** Spin inside a plate, and whoever's dreidel stays on it wins.
- **Final.** The two leaders spin three times, and the best of three wins the last sufganiyah.

## How do you make Hanukkah bingo?

Draw a five-by-five board, and write a Hanukkah word in every square, in a different order on each board. A host draws words, and the first to complete a row, a column or a diagonal shouts bingo.

Here is a ready board. Copy it onto a sheet of paper, and let every player rearrange the words as they like. For kids who can't read yet, you can draw instead of writing.

| B | I | N | G | O |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dreidel | Hanukkiah | Shamash | Latke | Sufganiyah |
| Jug of oil | Maccabees | Mattathias | Judah | Modi'in |
| Miracle | Kislev | ⭐ Free | Light | Hanukkah gelt |
| Maoz Tzur | Eight | Jam | Wick | Candle |
| Powdered sugar | The Temple | Menorah | Bravery | Darkness |

A family upgrade: swap five squares for sentences that get said at your home every Hanukkah, like "Who ate the last one?" or "Careful, the oil is hot". Mark them every time someone really says them.

## Which Hanukkah games suit kids?

Games with movement, searching and messy hands. They suit ages four and up, and most of them also work at a class party.

**4. Hanukkah gelt treasure hunt.** Hide chocolate coins around the house, with a clue at every station that leads to the next one. For little kids, draw the clues.

**11. Sufganiyot decorating contest.** Sufganiyot with no filling, bowls of jam, chocolate and sprinkles, and five minutes on the clock. Grandma is the judge.

**13. Drawing a hanukkiah with your eyes closed.** Everyone draws a hanukkiah with nine candles without looking. Hang up all the drawings, and vote for the best one and the funniest one.

**12. Eight tasks for eight candles.** A small task for every evening: sing a Hanukkah song in a robot voice, tell a joke, say something nice about the person sitting next to you.

**6. Hanukkah riddles.** "I have one leg and four sides, who am I?" Write a few riddles about Hanukkah objects, from easy to hard.

## Which Hanukkah games suit adults and teens?

Games of guessing, memory and light competition that don't require getting up from the sofa. They work at an evening with friends, at a candle lighting in the office and at a get-together of the whole extended family.

**7. Hanukkah charades.** Act out without words: "frying latkes", "spilling oil on the floor", "looking for the shamash in the box".

**8. Who am I?** Stick a note on everyone's forehead with a word from the holiday or the name of someone in the family. Only yes or no questions are allowed.

**9. Guess the hummed song.** Hum a Hanukkah song without the words, and the others guess. Each song is allowed only once, and after "Sevivon Sov Sov Sov" it gets hard.

**10. Blind sufganiyot tasting.** Cut sufganiyot with different fillings into small pieces, and guess blindfolded: jam, chocolate, dulce de leche or vanilla.

**15. Whose memory is it?** Everyone writes a memory from a Hanukkah long ago on a note. Read them out without names, and guess whose story it is.

**14. Hanukkah pass the parcel.** Hanukkah gelt in the middle, and a task or a question in every layer. Here are [60 tasks and questions for pass the parcel](/blog/pass-the-parcel-tasks/).
