A hen do brings together the bride's school friends, her uni friends, her colleagues, her sister and maybe her mum and future mother-in-law. Some of them have never met. The right game gets everyone laughing together in the first half hour. Here are 25 hen party games for every kind of group, plus 30 ready-made questions about the bride.

## What games do you play at a hen party?

The most popular hen party games are about the bride: Mr and Mrs, a quiz about her, and two truths and a lie. Add a game that suits where you are, like bingo in a pub or "What am I" on a night in.

A plan that works for most hen dos:

1. **Start with food,** a brunch, an afternoon tea or a dinner, so everyone has time to meet.
2. **Play an icebreaker and one game about the bride** while you're still at the table.
3. **Then the main event:** a class, a spa, a night out or a night in.

## Which hen do games involve the groom?

Games with the groom use his answers, recorded in advance, so he's part of the day without being there. Mr and Mrs is the classic, and "who said it" and the groom's job list are quick extras.

**1. Mr and Mrs.** Before the hen do, ask the groom 20 questions about the bride and film his answers. At the hen do, the bride guesses each answer before you play his clip. We've written [100 Mr and Mrs quiz questions](/blog/mr-and-mrs-quiz-questions/) with a full guide to running it.

**2. Who said it.** Collect ten quotes from the groom and ten from the bride, like "I'd never go camping". Read each one out, and the bride guesses who said it.

**3. Groom's job list.** Each hen writes one job the groom must do in the first year of marriage, like "learn to make her coffee properly". The bride reads them out and picks her favourite, and the list goes to the groom.

## What are good icebreaker games for a hen party?

Good icebreakers get people who've never met talking about the one person they have in common: the bride. Keep them short, and play them at the start, while everyone is still finding their seats.

**5. Two truths and a lie.** Each hen shares three short stories about the bride, and the group guesses which one never happened.

**6. How I met the bride.** Go round the table and each hen tells the story of how she met the bride, in one minute. Vote for the best story.

**7. Name game.** Each hen gets a sticker with a nickname from the bride's life, like "Her Sister" or "Year 9 Science". Hens must find out who's who without asking directly.

## Which classy hen do games work for all ages?

Classy games are built on memories and family stories rather than drinking or dares. Advice cards, guess her age and a love story timeline let every generation shine, and they keep the mums and grans laughing.

**8. Advice cards.** Each hen writes one piece of marriage advice and one date idea. The bride keeps the cards.

**9. Guess her age.** Show ten photos of the bride from different years, and the hens guess how old she was in each. Mums and grans usually win this one.

**10. Love story timeline.** Hand out cards with moments from the couple's story, like the first date, the first holiday and the proposal. The group puts them in order.

**11. Memory jar.** Each hen writes down a favourite memory with the bride. She reads a few out loud and takes the jar home.

**12. Loo roll dress.** Teams design a wedding dress out of toilet roll on one of their hens, and the bride picks the winner. It works for every age and needs no drinks.

## What hen do games can you play in a pub, bar or restaurant?

In a pub or a restaurant, choose games that need only a card, a phone or nothing at all: hen bingo, "don't say wedding" and secret missions. They keep the group together without taking over the venue.

**13. Hen bingo.** Make bingo cards with things that might happen on the night: someone buys the bride a drink, a stranger gives marriage advice, the DJ plays a cheesy wedding song. First hen to fill a line wins.

**14. Don't say wedding.** Everyone gets five stickers at the start of the day. If you say "wedding", whoever catches you takes one of your stickers. Most stickers at the end wins.

**15. Secret missions.** Each hen draws a card with a small mission, like "get the bride to say the groom's name three times". Everyone reveals their mission at the end of the night.

**16. Photo challenge.** Recreate an old photo of the bride, find a sign with the couple's initials, or get a group shot with the bar staff.

**17. Scavenger hunt.** Give teams a list for the night, like a selfie with someone called the groom's name or a napkin signed by a stranger. First team back wins.

## What hen party games can you play at home on a night in?

A night in suits games that need a little space and a speaker: "What am I", pass the parcel and a lip sync battle. They work in a rented cottage, a lodge or the bride's living room.

**18. What am I.** Each hen sticks a note on her forehead with a word linked to the bride, like her favourite food or her first car. Players ask yes or no questions until they guess it.

**19. Pass the parcel.** Wrap a small gift in layers, with a task or a question about the bride in each layer. When the music stops, the hen holding the parcel opens one layer and does what it says.

**20. Lip sync battle.** Pairs pick a song and perform it, and the bride judges. The bride and her chief bridesmaid go last.

**21. Mocktail contest.** Teams invent a drink named after the couple, and the bride picks the winner.

**22. Never have I ever.** Players hold up five fingers and lower one for each thing they've done. Keep it clean if family are there, and let the bride go first.

## Which hen do games don't involve drinking?

Games without drinking focus on stories, memories and making something together: advice cards, the loo roll dress, the memory jar and a mocktail contest. They work for pregnant hens, hens who don't drink and daytime hen dos.

Every game in the classy section above works without a drop, and so do the icebreakers and the quiz about the bride. If some of the group are drinking and some aren't, avoid games where drinking is the forfeit, and use a small task instead, like telling a story about the bride.

## What hen do games can you play on a train or abroad?

On a train, a coach or a plane, pick games that need no props and can be played from your seats: finish the lyric, would you rather and most likely to. They also travel well to a hen do abroad.

**23. Finish the lyric.** One hen plays or sings the first line of a song, and the others race to finish it. End with the couple's own song.

**24. Would you rather.** Ask the bride wedding-themed questions, like "would you rather have the first dance or give a speech", and the group guesses her answer first.

**25. Most likely to.** Read a prompt, like "most likely to be late to the wedding", and everyone points at a hen on three. Add prompts about the bride and the groom.

## Who usually pays for the hen party?

Usually the hens split the cost between them, and each pays for her own travel and room. Decide together whether to share the bride's costs too, and agree it before anyone books.

[Sharing the costs among the guests is the custom in most English-speaking countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelorette_party), but there's no fixed rule. A few tips to keep it friendly:

- **Share a rough budget early,** before anyone books travel or a cottage.
- **Offer a cheaper option,** like joining for one day instead of the whole weekend.
- **Collect money in one place,** so the organiser isn't chasing payments.
- **Don't make the games cost anything extra.** All 25 games here need only pens, cards or a phone.

Planning usually falls to the chief bridesmaid. [Bridebook's guide to the hen do](https://bridebook.com/uk/article/introduction-hen-do) is written for her, and it's a good checklist if it's your first time organising one.
