Mr and Mrs is the quiz every hen do seems to include: the bride guesses how the groom answered questions about her, and the room finds out how well she really knows him. It works just as well at a stag do, at the wedding itself or at a ruby or golden anniversary. Here are 100 Mr and Mrs quiz questions, sorted by occasion, and everything you need to run the game.

## What is the Mr and Mrs quiz and how does it work?

Mr and Mrs is a couples quiz: one partner answers questions about the other in advance, and the other guesses what they said. Every matching answer scores a point, and every mismatch gets a laugh.

The name comes from [Mr and Mrs, a long-running British TV game show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr_and_Mrs_(game_show)) in which married couples were tested on how well they knew each other. In the US the same party game is called the newlywed game, and our [100 newlywed game questions](/blog/newlywed-game-questions/) have more ideas for engaged and married couples.

There are three common ways to play:

1. **The video reveal.** One partner answers on camera before the event. At the party, the other guesses, then you play the clip.
2. **Back to back.** The couple sit back to back and each holds up a card, a paddle or a shoe with a name on it. Great at the wedding itself.
3. **Him or her.** For "who is most likely to" questions, both answer at the same time by pointing or holding up a sign. The room sees straight away if they agree.

## How many Mr and Mrs questions should you ask?

Ask 15 to 20 questions, which takes about 15 minutes. Choose about half from the couple's story and the rest from the funny and "who is most likely to" lists, and end on a sweet one.

[WeddingWire suggests keeping the shoe game to about 10 minutes](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-ideas/wedding-shoe-game), and the same rule of thumb works for Mr and Mrs: after 20 questions people start drifting to the bar. If you have more questions you love, split them into two rounds, one early in the evening and one later.

Write a few spare questions on a separate card. If one falls flat, or the bride asks to skip it, the host can move straight on.

## How do you organise a Mr and Mrs quiz for a hen do?

Ask the groom 20 to 25 questions about the bride about a week before the hen do, and film his answers on your phone. At the hen do, read each question, let the bride guess, then play his answer.

Step by step:

1. **Choose the questions** from the lists below, and swap a few for in-jokes only your group will get.
2. **Interview the groom** a week ahead, ideally on video. Ask him to keep it secret, and to answer quickly without overthinking.
3. **Cut the video** into short clips, one per answer, or note the time of each answer so you can skip to it.
4. **At the hen do,** sit the bride somewhere everyone can see her, read each question, let her answer, then play the clip.
5. **Keep score.** Some groups give the bride a small forfeit for each wrong answer, like telling the story behind the question. Keep forfeits light if the mums are there.

The questions for the groom are in the hen do section below. For more games to play on the same day, see our [hen party games](/blog/hen-party-games/).

## Can you play Mr and Mrs at the wedding or a golden anniversary?

Yes: at the wedding, play after the speeches while everyone is still seated for the wedding breakfast, or as a warm-up for the evening do. At a ruby or golden anniversary, the questions look back on the years together.

[Bridebook says speeches traditionally come after the wedding breakfast](https://bridebook.com/uk/article/wedding-speech-order), which makes the gap between the speeches and the first dance a natural slot. Keep it to 10 or 12 "who is most likely to" questions, played back to back with paddles or shoes. Our [wedding shoe game questions](/blog/wedding-shoe-game-questions/) include a short script for whoever is holding the microphone.

For a ruby, golden or diamond anniversary:

96. What was the date of your wedding?
97. Where did you go on honeymoon?
98. What was your first car together?
99. What's the secret to a long marriage, in your partner's words?
100. Would you marry your partner again? (Both answer at once.)

Ask the children and grandchildren to send in a few questions beforehand. They know family stories the guests have never heard.

Here are four ways to run it, side by side:

| Occasion | Who guesses | What you need | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hen do | The bride | The groom's answers on video | 15 to 20 min |
| Stag do | The groom | The bride's answers | 10 to 15 min |
| Wedding | Both, back to back | Paddles or shoes, a mic | About 10 min |
| On phones | Every guest | A screen, the guests' phones | 15 to 20 min |

## How can all the guests play, not just the couple?

Let every guest guess each answer too, before the couple reveal it. Then everyone has a score, and the game ends with a winner among the guests: the person who knows the couple best.
