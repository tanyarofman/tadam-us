Not every New Year's Eve needs a ticket to a party. An evening at home, with the people you went through the year with, can be the one you remember, as long as there is more to it than the TV and a countdown. We have gathered here ten New Year's Eve games and thirty questions that sum up your year, not the year of the news.

## When is Sylvester 2026, and how is it different from Novy God?

The eve of the civil New Year falls on Thursday, December 31, 2026. Sylvester (סילבסטר) and Novy God (נובי גוד) are celebrated on the same night, and they are two different traditions of the same moment.

Novy God, "new year" in Russian, is according to [the Ministry of Education](https://pop.education.gov.il/new-immigrants/cultural-social-integration/novy-god/) a family holiday with no religious or ideological meaning, celebrated on the night between December 31 and January 1. It came to Israel with the immigrants from the former Soviet Union. The name Sylvester comes from Pope [Sylvester I, who died on December 31, 335](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%91%D7%A1%D7%98%D7%A8_%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F). Whatever you call this evening at your place, the games here suit it.

## What do you do on New Year's Eve at home?

Build an evening with three parts: a meal, a game that sums up the year, and a countdown. The table suggests a plan for the evening that you can copy.

| Time | What you do | How long |
|---|---|---|
| 20:00 | Dinner, and everyone brings one dish | An hour |
| 21:00 | Opening game: two truths and a lie about the year | 15 minutes |
| 21:30 | The quiz of our year | 20 minutes |
| 22:30 | Awards of the year and the gratitude jar | Half an hour |
| 23:30 | Time capsule and one resolution | 20 minutes |
| 23:55 | Countdown | Five minutes |

With young children, move everything three hours earlier and count down at nine. Choose the games according to the group: two or three are enough.

## Which games suit New Year's Eve?

Games whose material is the year of the people in the room: what happened to them, what made them laugh and what they are planning. Here are ten, from lighthearted to moving.

1. **The quiz of our year.** Everyone sends in three facts about their year in advance, and you build questions from them: "Which of us moved apartments twice?".
2. **Two truths and a lie about the year.** Three things that happened to you this year, one of which did not really happen. The others guess.
3. **Photo of the year.** Everyone sends one photo that sums up their year. You show them without names, guess whose each one is, and hear the story.
4. **Awards of the year.** Vote on funny categories: the longest voice message of the year, the excuse of the year, the dish of the year.
5. **Year bingo.** Each square holds something a person could have done this year: "started a class", "flew to a new destination", "changed jobs". You look around the room for who did what.
6. **Guess the song.** A playlist of the songs that accompanied the group this year. Play two seconds, and guess.
7. **The gratitude jar.** Everyone writes on a note one good thing that happened to them this year thanks to someone in the room. Read them out loud.
8. **Time capsule.** Everyone writes one prediction for next year, about themselves and about someone else. Seal them in an envelope, and open it at the end of next year.
9. **One resolution.** Everyone writes one resolution for the new year, with no name. Read them out, and guess whose each one is.
10. **An envelope for every hour.** A sealed envelope for each hour until midnight, and in each one a small task for everyone.

## Which year-in-review questions do you ask family and friends?

Open questions about moments, discoveries and people, which everyone answers in a sentence or two. They work around a table, going around, when everyone answers the same question.

1. What was your best moment this year?
2. What was the funniest thing that happened to you this year?
3. What did you learn this year that you did not know before?
4. Which new place did you discover?
5. What is your song of the year?
6. What is your series, movie or book of the year?
7. What was the best purchase you made?
8. What are you most proud of?
9. Who is the new person who came into your life this year?
10. What would you do differently?
11. What was the best meal you ate this year?
12. What did you put off all year, and do in the end?
13. Which new habit did you pick up?
14. Who do you want to thank for this year?
15. If your year were a movie, what would it be called?

## Which "which of us" questions suit a year in review?

Questions whose answer is one of the people in the room, with everyone pointing at them at the same moment. They are funniest when the answer is obvious to everyone except the person in question.

16. Which of us flew the most this year?
17. Who went through the most hobbies?
18. Who sent the longest voice message?
19. Who was late the most times?
20. Who surprised everyone this year?
21. Who took the most photos of food?
22. Who watched the most series?
23. Who changed the most this year?
24. Who will keep their New Year's resolution, and who will crack by February?
25. Who will host New Year's Eve next year?

## Which year-in-review questions suit a team at work?

Questions that sum up the team's year and not the company's: moments, thanks and lessons. They suit the last meeting of December or an end-of-year holiday toast.

26. Which of the team's achievements will we remember most from this year?
27. What was the funniest moment we had this year?
28. What did we learn from the thing that worked the least?
29. Who on the team deserves a thank you that has not been said yet?
30. What is the one thing we will want to do differently next year?

## How do you turn New Year's resolutions into a game?

Write just one resolution, tell everyone about it, and set a time to check in. A resolution said out loud in front of friends is a resolution with witnesses.

The odds are not as bad as people tend to think. According to the psychologist John Norcross, who has studied New Year's resolutions, [40 to 46 percent of people who make a resolution are still keeping it after six months](https://www.wusf.org/2012-12-28/making-resolutions-that-stick). In the "One resolution" game every player gets a partner, and the two check on each other at the end of January. You open the time capsule on the next New Year's Eve.

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** Eight friends in the living room, December 31. In the awards of the year, the "excuse of the year" category goes unanimously to the one who canceled a trip because of "something to do with the cat's uncle". In "Photo of the year" nobody recognizes the photo of a trail in the mountains, until the quietest friend in the group says that this year she hiked the Israel National Trail alone.
