The newlywed game is simple: one partner answers a question about the other, and the room finds out whether they agree. It works because every question is really a small story about the couple. Here are 100 questions to choose from, sorted by crowd and occasion, and everything you need to host the game.

## What is the newlywed game and how do you play it?

The newlywed game is a couples quiz: each partner answers questions about the other, and you count how many answers match. It is named after [the American TV game show The Newlywed Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Newlywed_Game), which first aired in 1966.

The basic version for a party:

1. **Before the event,** one partner answers the questions privately. Write the answers down, or record a short video.
2. **At the event,** the host reads each question to the other partner, who guesses what their partner said.
3. **The reveal.** The host reads the real answer, or plays the video. A match is a point.

A faster version needs no preparation at all. Seat the couple back to back, read a question, and both answer at the same moment on a whiteboard or an answer card. The room sees right away when the answers don't match, and that's where the laughs are.

## How many newlywed game questions should you ask?

Ask 15 to 20 questions, which takes 10 to 15 minutes. Pick about half from the couple's story and the rest from the funny and clean lists, and end with a sweet one.

Wedding guides give the same advice for the shoe game, the reception version of this game: [WeddingWire suggests keeping it to about 10 minutes](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-ideas/wedding-shoe-game), which is 15 to 20 questions. Past that point guests start checking their phones. If you have more questions you love, split them into two short rounds, one before the meal and one after.

Write a few spare questions on a separate card. If a question falls flat, or the couple asks to skip one, the host moves on without a pause.

## How do you host the newlywed game at a shower, rehearsal dinner or reception?

Pick a host, prepare the questions and answers a week ahead, and schedule the game for a calm moment, after the meal and before the dancing. The table below compares three ways to run it.

| | Answer cards | The shoe game | Everyone on phones |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who plays** | One partner answers ahead, the other guesses | The couple, back to back | The couple and all the guests |
| **What you need** | Questions, answers collected in advance, a host | Two shoes, a mic, 15 to 20 questions | A screen and the guests' phones |
| **How long** | 15 to 20 minutes | About 10 minutes | 15 to 25 minutes |
| **Best for** | Bridal shower, bachelorette | Reception, rehearsal dinner | Any event where guests want to play too |

At the reception, the shoe game is the classic: the couple holds one shoe of each, and raises the shoe of whoever the answer is. We wrote a full guide with [60 wedding shoe game questions and a short MC script](/blog/wedding-shoe-game-questions/).

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** At a rehearsal dinner for 40, the maid of honor asks "Who said 'I love you' first?" Both partners raise their own shoe. The groom's grandfather calls out from table three that he heard it first, on the phone, two Thanksgivings ago. The room votes, the grandfather wins, and the couple gets a point anyway.

A few hosting tips:

- **Check every question with someone close to the couple.** A joke that works with college friends can hurt in front of family.
- **Keep the scores light.** Nobody should leave the game feeling they don't know their partner.
- **End on a warm question,** like "When did you know this was the one?", so the game closes on a toast, not a punchline.

## How can all the guests play, not just the couple?

Let the guests guess each answer too, before the couple reveals it. Then everyone has a score, and the game ends with a winner among the guests: the person who knows the couple best.
