Most office holiday parties look the same: a speech, a drink, some snacks, and within half an hour people drift back to their desks or out the door. One good game changes that. Here are 15 office Christmas party games, for large groups and small teams, and how to make the party work for everyone on the team.

## What are fun games to play at an office Christmas party?

The best office party games need no special talent, no standing up alone in front of everyone, and only a few minutes to explain. The table compares 15 of them by group size, time and prep.

Pick one main game and one or two short ones. Three games is plenty for a two-hour party.

## How do you play White Elephant at work?

Everyone brings one wrapped gift within a set spending limit and draws a number. In order, each player opens a new gift or steals one that's already open, and whoever loses a gift picks again.

The [White Elephant gift exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_elephant_gift_exchange) is also called Yankee Swap in New England and Dirty Santa in much of the South, where stealing is part of the fun. A few rules keep it friendly at work:

1. **Set a spending limit** and stick to it, so nobody feels awkward.
2. **Limit steals.** A gift is "frozen" after its third steal.
3. **No immediate steal-backs.** You can't take back a gift that was just taken from you.
4. **Player one gets the last move.** At the end, the first player can swap with anyone, because they had nothing to steal at the start.
5. **Keep it work-safe.** Funny is good; anything you wouldn't show your manager is not.

With more than 25 people, split into two or three circles, or the game drags.

## How do you run Secret Santa at work?

Each person draws a colleague's name in secret and buys them a gift within a set spending limit. At the party, gifts are handed out, and everyone guesses who their Secret Santa was.

- **Draw names two to three weeks ahead.** A free online name drawer lets remote colleagues join and keeps the draw secret.
- **Ask for a short wish list.** Three ideas, like "coffee, puzzles, anything with cats," take the stress out of shopping.
- **Make it opt-in.** Not everyone wants to buy a gift, and that's fine.
- **Add a guessing round.** Before anyone reveals themselves, everyone guesses their Santa. The best guesser wins a small prize.

## Which office party games work for large groups?

Choose games where everyone plays at the same time, not one person while 80 others watch. Team trivia, holiday bingo and a photo scavenger hunt all work with 100 people or more.

**Team trivia on a big screen.** Mix general holiday questions with questions about the team: "Who has worked here the longest?" or "Whose desk plant has survived three office moves?" Everyone answers from their phone, and the leaderboard does the scoring.

**Holiday bingo.** Fill the cards with things that happen at your party: "someone mentions the budget," "a manager dances," "someone wears antlers." The first full row wins.

**Photo scavenger hunt.** Teams of five get a list (a photo with the CEO, a human Christmas tree, the whole team in one elevator) and 30 minutes.

**Guess the baby photo.** Collect baby photos in advance, show them on the screen, and everyone guesses who's who. It works especially well when the team is new.

## Which office party games work for small teams?

With 5 to 20 people, you can play games that let everyone talk and tell a story. These work around one table or in a meeting room.

**Two truths and a lie.** Everyone shares three facts about themselves, and the team guesses the lie. Our [icebreaker questions for work](/blog/icebreaker-questions/) are a good source of prompts.

**Office awards.** Vote ahead of time on funny categories: longest email, strongest coffee, best Zoom background, most legendary typo.

**Holiday charades.** Act out "untangling the lights," "the last-minute deadline," or "the reply-all email."

**Gingerbread build-off.** Teams of three get a kit and 40 minutes to build the office building, or their dream office.

## What are 60-second challenge games for an office holiday party?

Quick one-minute challenges with household items, played one round at a time with a timer on the screen. They're loud, funny and need no skill.

- **Candy cane hook.** Hold a candy cane in your mouth and hook as many candy canes off the table as you can.
- **Ornament stack.** Stack plastic ornaments into a tower that stands for five seconds.
- **Unwrap with mittens.** Unwrap a small gift while wearing oven mitts.
- **Cookie face.** Move a cookie from your forehead to your mouth with no hands.
- **Ribbon wrap.** Wrap a teammate in ribbon like a present.

Run them as team relays, so the whole room is cheering instead of watching one person.

## How do you make a work holiday party inclusive?

Not everyone on your team celebrates Christmas, so call it a holiday party, keep the decorations and food general, and make attendance optional. Pick games anyone can play, like trivia about the team, instead of games that assume everyone knows Christmas traditions.

The [Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM)](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/hr-magazine/how-to-make-holiday-celebrations-inclusive) advises keeping holiday parties voluntary, choosing a date that doesn't clash with religious observances, and asking a diverse group of employees to help plan. A few practical points:

- **Offer good non-alcoholic drinks,** not just water. Some colleagues are driving, and some don't drink.
- **Check the date** against Hanukkah and other observances on your team.
- **Hold it during work hours** if many people have caregiving duties in the evening.

## How do you include remote employees in the holiday party?

Choose games that work from a phone or a laptop, put the main screen on a video call, and send a small box to remote colleagues ahead of time. That way people at home play the same games at the same moment.

It matters, especially for hybrid teams. [Gallup data reported by Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/08/24/work-bff-more-important-than-ever-declining/) found that only about 2 in 10 US employees have a best friend at work, and that hybrid employees were the least likely: 17% in 2022, down from 22% in 2019.

- Run Secret Santa with an online name drawer and ship the gifts.
- Share the screen for trivia and bingo, and make sure remote players can hear the host.
- Give remote colleagues their own team, so they're competing, not just watching.

## Why does a trivia game about your team work so well?

A general quiz feels like a test, but a quiz about the team feels like a party. Every question is a small story about someone in the room, and every answer starts a conversation.

The same [Gallup data](https://fortune.com/2022/08/24/work-bff-more-important-than-ever-declining/) found that employees with a best friend at work were more than twice as likely to be satisfied with their job: 32% against 15%. A game where people discover things about each other is a fast way to build those connections. It also brings in quiet colleagues, who won't give a speech but love seeing their story on the screen.
