Some games need no explanation. A circle, music, a parcel passing from hand to hand, and everyone hoping the music will stop right when it reaches them. What turns pass the parcel from one more game into a moment people remember is what's written on the notes. We've gathered 60 ready-made tasks and questions here, by age.

## What is pass the parcel, and how do you play?

Pass the parcel is a party game in which a prize wrapped in many layers goes around a circle while music plays. When the music stops, whoever is holding the parcel removes one layer, and you keep going until the last layer.

It is known as a [classic British party game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pass_the_parcel). In the original version, a small prize waits in every layer. In the version on this page, every layer holds a note with a task or a question, and the prize is saved for whoever opens the last layer.

## How do you prepare a pass the parcel?

Choose a prize, wrap it in one layer per player, and attach a note to every layer. The preparation takes about half an hour.

1. **Choose a main prize.** Something every one of the players would be happy to get.
2. **Write the notes.** A task or a question on each note. Mix easy ones with challenging ones.
3. **Wrap from the inside out.** Layer, note, layer, note. Change the paper color from layer to layer, so it's clear where a layer ends.
4. **Don't overdo the tape.** One piece per layer, otherwise every turn takes a minute.
5. **Prepare a playlist.** Someone with their back to the circle stops the music, so there are no complaints.

## How many layers do you need, and what goes inside a pass the parcel?

One layer per player plus two or three spare, and one prize in the middle. The table shows what suits each age.

| Audience | How many layers | What's on the notes | What goes inside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kindergarten to 2nd grade | 8 to 12 | Movement and sound tasks, no reading | A small sweet in every layer and a prize in the middle |
| 3rd to 6th grade | 12 to 20 | Short tasks and questions | A prize in the middle, and possibly two surprises along the way |
| Teens | 12 to 20 | Questions, "Pass it to whoever…" notes and group tasks | One prize that suits everyone |
| Adults | 10 to 15 | Funny tasks and personal questions | A humorous prize or a gift voucher |

## Which pass the parcel tasks suit kids?

Short movement, sound and acting tasks that take up to half a minute and don't embarrass anyone. You can copy these fifteen tasks exactly as they are.

1. Hop on one foot ten times.
2. Make an animal sound, and let everyone guess which animal.
3. Sing a line from a birthday song in a robot voice.
4. Say "She sells seashells by the seashore" three times, fast.
5. Act out a job without words, until someone guesses it.
6. Walk like a penguin around the circle.
7. Make your funniest face for ten seconds.
8. Count down from ten to one in a whisper.
9. Give a high five to everyone sitting in the circle.
10. Dance for ten seconds with no music.
11. Say something nice to the person sitting on your right.
12. Act out a sport without words, until someone guesses it.
13. Stand like a statue until someone manages to make you laugh.
14. Spin around three times and then walk in a straight line.
15. Make up a short dance, and everyone copies you.

## What questions can you ask in pass the parcel?

Short questions with no right answer, the kind every child can answer and that reveal something about them. They suit 3rd to 6th graders and teens too.

16. If you could eat only one food for the rest of your life, what would it be?
17. Which superpower would you choose?
18. What's the funniest thing that happened to you this year?
19. Where would you fly tomorrow morning?
20. Which animal is most like you, and why?
21. What would you do with a million dollars?
22. Which school subject would you cancel, and which would you add?
23. Which song do you sing in the shower?
24. What's the first thing you do when you get home?
25. What's the best present you ever got?
26. What are you best at?
27. Which cartoon movie character would you like to meet?
28. What would you change in class if you were the teacher for one day?
29. What's your dream for the summer vacation?
30. Which food can you least stand?

## Which "Pass the parcel to whoever…" notes suit teens?

Notes that send the parcel to someone else in the group, based on something good or funny. That way, even someone the music keeps skipping gets a turn.

It's worth sticking to complimentary notes. A note like "Pass it to whoever is the most annoying" sounds funny when you write it, and less so when you read it out loud.

31. Pass it to whoever always makes you laugh.
32. Pass it to whoever knows how to keep a secret.
33. Pass it to whoever always arrives last.
34. Pass it to whoever you'd take with you to a desert island.
35. Pass it to whoever has the most contagious laugh.
36. Pass it to whoever always knows what the homework is.
37. Pass it to whoever is best at picking songs.
38. Pass it to whoever will be famous one day.
39. Pass it to whoever you met most recently in the group.
40. Pass it to whoever is wearing the most colors today.

## Which funny pass the parcel tasks suit adults?

Tasks that involve the rest of the room, that are funny without humiliating anyone, and that don't require getting up from your chair if you don't want to. They work at milestone birthdays, team evenings and get-togethers with friends.

41. Tell a dad joke, and don't be the first to laugh.
42. Imitate someone in the room, until people guess who it is.
43. Sing the chorus of the first song that comes to mind.
44. Raise a short toast to the person sitting on your left.
45. Tell everyone about your most embarrassing moment of the past year.
46. Show everyone the last photo in your phone's gallery (you're allowed to skip once).
47. Speak in an accent of your choice until the next turn.
48. Give a half-minute speech on "why I'm the best parker in this room".
49. Act out a movie without words, until people guess it.
50. Send an "I love you" message to a family member right now, and read out the reply.
51. Share something nobody in the room knows about you.
52. Pick someone in the room and invent an election slogan for them.

## How do you make a personal pass the parcel about the guest of honor?

Swap some of the notes for questions about the person you're celebrating. Whoever answers correctly stays in the game, and whoever gets it wrong does a task.

This is the upgrade that turns pass the parcel from a general activity into a game about the bat mitzvah girl, the birthday boy or the bride. Ask family and close friends to send answers in advance, so there's someone to settle it. Here are eight sample questions:

53. What dish does he (or she) always order?
54. Which phrase is most identified with him?
55. What is he most afraid of?
56. What was his childhood nickname?
57. Which song gets him onto the dance floor in a second?
58. Which hobby did he start and drop after a week?
59. Where was his most memorable trip?
60. What did he want to be when he grew up?

## How do you play pass the parcel when there are a lot of guests?

Up to 20 players, one circle is enough. Beyond that, it's better to split into several circles, or to move to a game in which everyone takes part in every question.

In pass the parcel, only the person the music stops at gets a turn. With 15 players that's part of the fun, and with 80 guests most people are just watching. If the questions about the guest of honor were the part that worked best for you, you can build the whole evening on them.
