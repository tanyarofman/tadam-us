The rehearsal dinner is the calmest party of the wedding weekend: a smaller crowd, no schedule to keep, and time to actually talk. It's also where the funniest stories about the couple usually come out. Here are 25 ideas, from a backyard barbecue to a dinner cruise, and what to do besides eat.

## What is the purpose of a rehearsal dinner?

A rehearsal dinner thanks the people who are part of the wedding and lets the two families get to know each other before the big day. It usually follows the ceremony rehearsal, the night before the wedding.

It's also the one wedding event with room for long toasts, old stories and inside jokes. The wedding day runs on a timeline; the rehearsal dinner can run on stories.

## Who is invited to the rehearsal dinner?

Everyone who is part of the ceremony is invited: the wedding party, the officiant, readers, the parents and their partners. Many couples also invite grandparents, siblings, close family and the wedding party's plus-ones.

The Knot says [anyone who's part of your wedding ceremony should be invited](https://www.theknot.com/content/who-should-be-invited-to-rehearsal-dinner), which includes:

- The wedding party: maid or matron of honor, best man, bridesmaids and groomsmen
- The flower girl and ring bearer, with their parents
- The officiant and any readers or ushers
- Both sets of parents, and usually siblings and grandparents
- The wedding party's plus-ones

Some couples widen the list to out-of-town guests, which turns the dinner into a welcome party. That's a bigger event with a bigger budget, so decide early.

## Which family usually pays for the rehearsal dinner?

Traditionally, the groom's family hosts and pays for the rehearsal dinner. Today it's often shared, paid by the couple or hosted by whichever family offers, and whoever pays usually gets a say in the guest list and the venue.

The Knot notes that [traditionally, the groom's family pays](https://www.theknot.com/content/mandatory-wedding-rehearsal-dinner), but that there are many options for hosts today. The tradition comes from the bride's family paying for the wedding itself, which is also less common than it used to be. Ask, don't assume: a parent who wants to host will usually offer.

## What are casual, inexpensive rehearsal dinner ideas?

Casual rehearsal dinners are often the most fun: a backyard barbecue, a pizza party, a taco bar or a picnic. Serve food family-style, skip the printed menus and spend the effort on toasts and one activity.

- **Backyard barbecue (1).** A family member's backyard, a grill and string lights.
- **Pizza party (2).** Order from the couple's favorite place, or rent a wood-fired pizza truck.
- **Taco bar (3).** Easy for a mixed crowd, with options for every diet.
- **Picnic in a park (12).** Blankets, sandwiches and lawn games, if the weather allows.
- **Late pizza after the rehearsal (25).** For couples who would rather save the budget for the wedding.

For a group of 20, a home-cooked or catered family-style meal in someone's home often feels more personal than a private room, and it leaves more of the budget for the wedding.

## What fun activities are there at a rehearsal dinner besides eating?

Toasts, a slideshow of the couple, and a game about them are the classic activities. Plan one or two between courses, so guests stay at their tables and the couple gets to hear every story.

- **Toasts and roasts (19).** Four or five speakers, two or three minutes each. [Toastmasters suggests keeping a toast to two or three minutes](https://www.toastmasters.org/resources/public-speaking-tips/how-to-give-a-toast).
- **Slideshow (18).** Photos of each partner growing up, then the two of them together.
- **Trivia about the couple (20).** Questions about how they met, their first trip and their worst fight, answered by guests in teams.
- **Shoe game preview (21).** A short round of the [shoe game](/blog/wedding-shoe-game-questions/), with the questions too wild for the reception.
- **Advice cards (22).** Guests write advice for the couple, to be read on their first anniversary.
- **Gifts for the wedding party (23).** The couple thanks their bridesmaids, groomsmen and parents.

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** A rehearsal dinner for 30 people in a family backyard. Between the barbecue and dessert, a trivia round asks where the couple had their first kiss. The groom's college roommate says "outside a bowling alley". The bride's mother says "in my kitchen". The bride admits both are true, on the same night, and the two families spend the rest of the evening comparing notes.
