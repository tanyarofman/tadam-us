## What are team building activities, and do they actually work?

Team building activities are short games, challenges or outings that help coworkers get to know each other and work better together. Research says they do work, mostly on how people feel about the team and how they cooperate.

A meta-analysis in *Small Group Research* that pooled earlier studies found that [team building has a positive, moderate effect on team outcomes](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/facultybib2000/1735), with the strongest links to how members feel about the team and how the team works together. It also matters for people: Gallup data reported by Fortune found that only [about 2 in 10 US employees have a best friend at work](https://fortune.com/2022/08/24/work-bff-more-important-than-ever-declining/), and those who do are far more satisfied with their jobs.

The activities that work best share three things:

- **They're short.** People join more easily when it ends before it gets awkward.
- **They're about the team.** Questions about colleagues beat generic games.
- **They're optional.** A forced activity builds resentment, not trust.

## Which 5-minute team building activities fit into a meeting?

Quick activities that need no setup fit into the first five minutes of any meeting. A one-word check-in, two truths and a lie, or a single fun question warms up the room without stealing the agenda.

**1. One-word check-in.** Everyone describes their week in one word. It takes a minute, and you learn who is swamped before you assign anything.

**2. Two truths and a lie.** One person shares three facts about themselves, and the team guesses the false one. Rotate one person per meeting so it stays short.

**3. Rose, bud, thorn.** Each person shares a win (rose), something they're looking forward to (bud) and a challenge (thorn). It works best in teams that already trust each other.

**4. Desert island pick.** Ask what one book, album or snack each person would take to a desert island. Light questions like this are easy for new hires.

**5. Thank-you round.** Each person thanks one colleague for something specific from the past week. It ends meetings on a good note.

**6. Question of the day.** Open with one question and go around the room. Our list of [80 icebreaker questions for work](/blog/icebreaker-questions/) has enough for a year of meetings.

## Which team building activities can you do in the office?

In the office, choose activities that need a table and a few supplies: a trivia game about the team, the marshmallow challenge, a scavenger hunt or a skill swap lunch. Each takes 30 to 60 minutes and costs little.

**7. Trivia about the team.** Collect fun facts from everyone in advance: first jobs, hidden talents, the strangest place they've lived. Then the team guesses who is who. It's the one game where every answer is about the people in the room.

**8. Marshmallow challenge.** Small teams get spaghetti, tape, string and one marshmallow, and race to build the tallest tower that holds the marshmallow on top. It shows fast how each team plans and shares ideas.

**9. Office scavenger hunt.** Hide clues around the office, or give a list of things to find and photograph. Mix departments in each team so people meet colleagues they rarely talk to.

**10. Skill swap lunch.** Over lunch, two or three people each teach a 10-minute skill: a spreadsheet trick, a recipe, how to fold a paper crane. People see colleagues as more than their job title.

**11. Escape room in a box.** Boxed escape room games let a team of 4 to 8 solve a mystery around a conference table. Run several boxes at once for a bigger group.

**12. Work charades.** Act out office words and moments without speaking: "the printer is jammed", "reply all", "the Monday meeting". It's silly in the best way.

**13. Story potluck.** Everyone brings a dish with a story behind it, like a family recipe or a dish from a trip. The stories do the team building.

**14. Puzzle race.** Give each team the same jigsaw puzzle, with a few pieces swapped between teams. They have to negotiate to finish, which turns a quiet game into a lively one.

## Which team building activities work outside the office?

Outside the office, pick one activity that everyone can do regardless of fitness: a volunteer day, a cooking class, an escape room or a picnic. Leaving the building signals that this time is different from a regular workday.

**15. Volunteer day.** Spend half a day at a food bank, a park cleanup or a local charity. Teams often say it's the outing they remember most.

**16. Cooking class.** Teams cook one dish each, then share the meal. Cooking needs real cooperation, and nobody needs to be an athlete.

**17. Escape room.** Groups of 4 to 8 solve puzzles against the clock. Book several rooms at the same time for a larger team, then compare times over drinks.

**18. Bowling or mini golf.** Easy, social and no skill needed. Mix the teams so managers and new hires play together.

**19. City scavenger hunt.** Teams follow clues through the neighborhood and send photo proof. Many cities have companies that run hunts, or you can write your own.

**20. Picnic and lawn games.** Cornhole, frisbee and a long lunch in a park. Low effort to plan and good for teams that just need time together.

## What are some good team games for adults?

Good team games for adults are quick, need no props and let people laugh without embarrassing anyone. Twenty questions, back-to-back drawing, a clean version of never have I ever and the count to 20 game all fit.

**21. 20 questions.** See the next section for how to run it.

**22. Back-to-back drawing.** Pairs sit back to back. One describes a simple picture, the other draws it without looking. It's a fast lesson in clear communication.

**23. Never have I ever (work edition).** Keep it clean and about work and travel: "Never have I ever sent an email to the wrong person." Players hold up fingers and lower one for each thing they've done.

**24. Count to 20.** The group counts from 1 to 20 out loud, one number per person, with no order and no signals. If two people speak at once, start over. It sounds easy and isn't.

## What is the 20 questions game for team building?

In 20 questions, one person thinks of something and the team has up to 20 yes or no questions to guess it. For work, the secret answer can be a past project, a product, or a city a teammate has lived in.

How to run it:

1. One player picks a secret answer and tells the facilitator.
2. The team asks yes or no questions, one at a time. Keep count out loud.
3. If the team guesses within 20 questions, they win the round. If not, the player reveals the answer.
4. Rotate the player each round.

The work version teaches people to ask sharper questions, and secret answers about teammates turn it into a getting-to-know-you game.

## Which team building activities work for large groups?

For 30 people or more, run activities in small tables or teams that compete with each other: a trivia tournament, coworker bingo or a paper plane contest. Everyone plays at once, and nobody stands in front of the whole room alone.

**25. Table trivia tournament.** Each table is a team, and a host reads questions from a stage or a screen. Mix general questions with questions about the company. Our [100 trivia questions for adults](/blog/general-knowledge-trivia-questions/) cover the general part.

**26. Coworker bingo.** Each bingo square says something like "has worked here 10 years" or "speaks three languages". People walk around to find a colleague for each square. It's the easiest way to mix departments at a big event.

**27. Paper plane contest.** Every team designs a plane and competes for distance, time in the air and best design. Cheap, fast and loud.

## Which team building activities work for remote and hybrid teams?

For remote and hybrid teams, keep activities short, run them at the start of regular calls, and choose games that work on a video screen. Online trivia, desk show and tell and virtual coffee pairs all work well.

**28. Online trivia.** A host shares questions on screen and everyone answers on their own device. Questions about the team work as well online as in the room.

**29. Desk show and tell.** Each person shows one object from their desk or home and tells its story. It's a window into colleagues' lives you never get in an office.

**30. Virtual coffee pairs.** Pair people at random for a 20-minute video chat with no agenda. Change the pairs every week or two.

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** A finance team of 14 runs a 30-minute trivia game about itself. Question 6 asks who once worked as a lifeguard, and nobody picks the quiet analyst who joined last month. He turns out to have spent four summers on a beach, and by the end of the week half the team has asked him about it.

## How do you choose an activity for your goal?

Start from what the team needs: getting to know each other, communicating better, or simply having fun after a hard stretch. Then pick an activity that fits the time and place you have.

- **New team or new hires:** activities 1, 2, 7 and 26. They're about the people.
- **Communication problems:** activities 8, 14, 22 and 24. They need people to explain and listen.
- **After a hard project:** activities 16, 18 and 20. Pure fun and a break.
- **Remote team:** activities 28 to 30, plus any 5-minute idea at the start of calls.

Before you book anything, ask the team what they'd enjoy. If an activity takes place during work hours, the federal rule is that time at meetings and similar activities [counts as paid work time unless all four conditions are met](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/785.27): it's outside working hours, voluntary, not directly related to the job, and involves no productive work. Check with HR if you're unsure.
