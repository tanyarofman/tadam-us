Wedding receptions are long, and most of the time guests are eating, waiting or dancing. A good game fills the gap in between: ten minutes in which the whole room laughs at the same thing and learns something new about the couple. Here are 20 reception games, when to play each one, and how to keep them short.

## What are fun games to play at a wedding?

The most fun wedding games are short, about the couple and easy to join: the shoe game, a trivia round about how they met, and table games guests can play between courses. Choose one main game for the whole room and two or three table games.

| Game | When | Players | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Shoe game | After dinner | Couple | 10 min |
| 2. Couple trivia | Between courses | All | 15 min |
| 3. Table trivia cards | During dinner | Tables | Ongoing |
| 4. Kissing game | During dinner | Tables | Ongoing |
| 5. Anniversary dance | Dance floor | Couples | 5 min |
| 6. Who knows them best | After dinner | All | 10 min |

| 8. Photo scavenger hunt | All night | All | Ongoing |

A few of the favorites:

- **Kissing game (4).** Instead of clinking glasses, a table has to do something (sing a love song, name the couple's first date spot) to get the couple to kiss.
- **Anniversary dance (5).** All married couples dance, and the DJ calls out years: couples married less than 5 years sit down, then 10, then 20. The last couple standing gives the newlyweds advice.
- **Centerpiece giveaway (15).** The guest at each table whose birthday is closest to the wedding date takes the centerpiece home.
- **Table name trivia (9).** Each table is named after a place in the couple's story, with a card explaining it and a question about it.

## What couple games can you play at a wedding reception?

The shoe game is the classic: the couple sits back to back, each holding one of their own shoes and one of their partner's, and raises a shoe to answer each question. A newlywed quiz and a "who knows them best" trivia round are the other favorites.

WeddingWire suggests keeping [the shoe game to no more than 10 minutes, about 15 to 20 questions](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-ideas/wedding-shoe-game). That's long enough for a few big laughs and short enough to keep the dance floor waiting. For ready-made questions, see our [shoe game questions](/blog/wedding-shoe-game-questions/).

Other couple games:

- **Newlywed quiz.** The MC asks each partner questions about the other, and the room checks the answers.
- **Who knows them best (6).** Guests answer questions about the couple, and the winner is the person who knows them best. Often a grandparent.
- **Two truths and a lie.** Each partner tells three stories, and the guests guess the lie.

## What are good questions for a bride and groom game?

Good questions are specific and a little funny: who said "I love you" first, who takes longer to get ready, who would survive longer on a desert island. Avoid anything that could embarrass a partner in front of their grandmother.

Questions that usually get a laugh:

- Who said "I love you" first?
- Who is the better cook?
- Who takes longer to get ready?
- Who's more likely to get lost, even with GPS?
- Who apologizes first after an argument?
- Who spends more on shoes?
- Who's the bigger baby when they're sick?
- Who will be the stricter parent?
- Who's the better driver?
- Who planned most of this wedding?

## How do you keep guests entertained at a wedding reception?

Keep the timeline moving, give guests something to do during the waits, and plan one moment that's about the couple. Table games, a photo scavenger hunt and lawn games fill the gaps, and one main game brings the room together.

A reception is long: WeddingWire describes [a four- or five-hour reception](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-ideas/how-long-is-a-wedding-reception) as typical for a full dinner with dancing, and The Knot's [2026 Real Weddings Study](https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/the-knot-worldwide-unveils-2026-real-weddings-study) puts the average guest count at 117. That's a lot of people who don't all know each other, for a lot of hours.

- **Cocktail hour:** lawn games and a guest scavenger hunt ("find someone who's been to Japan").
- **During dinner:** table trivia cards about the couple, and the kissing game.
- **After dinner:** one main game for the whole room, then the dancing.
- **All night:** a photo scavenger hunt and an advice card station.

## When during the reception should games happen?

Play the main game after dinner and before the dancing, when every guest is seated and the room is still fresh. Table games fit during dinner, and lawn games fit the cocktail hour.

- **Don't start with a game.** Guests are hungry and still finding their seats.
- **Don't interrupt the dancing.** Once the dance floor is full, keep it full.
- **Do play between courses or right after dinner.** It's the only moment the whole room is seated and facing the same way.
- **Ask the DJ or MC to run it.** They have the microphone, the music cues and the timeline.

> **Example scenario (not a real event):** A reception for 120 guests. Right after dinner, the MC runs a 12-minute trivia game about the couple. The question "Where did they go on their first trip together" gets the bride's whole table shouting "camping!" The answer is a theme park, because the camping trip was canceled by rain. The groom's grandmother wins the game and gets the first dance with him.

## What games work at the after party?

At the after party, guests want something looser: karaoke, card games, a dance off or a round of trivia about the night itself. Keep it optional, so the guests who want to talk can talk.

- **Karaoke (19).** The couple picks the first song.
- **Card games (18).** A few decks at each table for the small groups that form late at night.
- **Dance off (14).** The wedding party against the rest.
- **Trivia about the wedding.** Who cried first? Who caught the bouquet? A quick round with a small prize.
