The shoe game is one of the few reception games that needs almost nothing: two chairs, two pairs of shoes and a list of questions. Done right, it takes ten minutes and gives the room its biggest laugh of the night. Here are 60 questions to choose from, and everything you need to run it, from the opening line to the last question.

## What is the wedding shoe game?

The shoe game is a reception game in which the couple sits back to back, each holding one of their own shoes and one of their partner's. The MC reads a question, and each partner raises the shoe of whoever the answer is.

Neither partner can see what the other raised, but the guests can. When both raise the same shoe, the room cheers. When they raise different shoes, the room laughs, and the couple turns around to find out why. [WeddingWire's guide to the shoe game](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-ideas/wedding-shoe-game) describes the same setup: the newlyweds answer without seeing each other's response.

The shoe game is a cousin of the newlywed game, where partners answer questions about each other. ## How many questions should you ask in the shoe game?

Ask 15 to 20 questions. That's enough for a few matches, a few surprises and a strong ending, before the guests drift back to their drinks.

[WeddingWire recommends no more than 10 minutes](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-ideas/wedding-shoe-game), which is about 15 to 20 questions. Lists online run much longer ([The Knot alone has 102](https://www.theknot.com/content/shoe-game-wedding-reception-game-questions)), so treat any list, including ours, as a menu: choose the questions that fit this couple, and drop the rest.

A good mix for 18 questions: 10 clean questions, 6 funny ones, 1 about the wedding day itself, and 1 sweet closing question.

## How long does the shoe game take at a wedding?

Plan on 10 minutes, plus 2 minutes to set up the chairs and introduce the game. Each question takes about half a minute to read, answer and laugh at.

The table shows how to fit it into the reception:

| | What works |
|---|---|
| **When** | After dinner and before the dancing, or after the cake cutting while guests are still seated |
| **How many questions** | 15 to 20 |
| **How long** | About 10 minutes, plus 2 minutes of setup |
| **Who reads** | The DJ or MC, or a maid of honor or best man with a mic |
| **Where** | Two chairs back to back in the middle of the dance floor, facing the guests |
| **What else you need** | A printed question list for the reader, and a copy for the DJ's timeline |

Tell the DJ or planner the exact slot in advance. If dinner runs late, the shoe game is the easiest thing to shorten: cut it to 10 questions and keep the closing one.

## How do you introduce the shoe game?

The MC calls the couple to two chairs on the dance floor, explains the rules in two sentences, and asks a warm-up question. The whole introduction takes under a minute.

A short script the MC can read as is:

> "Ladies and gentlemen, it's time to find out how well these two really know each other. They're sitting back to back, each holding one of their own shoes and one of their partner's. I'll ask a question, and they'll raise the shoe of whoever the answer is. No peeking, no talking, and no help from the crowd. Let's start with an easy one: who said 'I love you' first?"

After the warm-up, the MC can read the rest at a steady pace. Pause a second after each reveal, so the guests have time to react.

## How do you end the shoe game?

End with one warm question that both partners will answer the same way, like who fell in love first. Ask the couple to raise both shoes, or each other's, then turn the chairs around for a kiss.

Some closing questions that work:

- Who fell in love first?
- Who's the luckiest person in the room tonight?
- Who's going to love the other one more in 50 years?
- Who's going to say "I love you" first tomorrow morning?

The MC can close with: "Give it up for the newlyweds!" Then cue the next song, so the energy goes straight to the dance floor.

## What if the couple doesn't want to be on the spot?

Turn the spotlight from the couple to the guests. Guests answer questions about the couple, and the couple watches, laughs and reveals the right answers.

Not every couple enjoys being on a stage for ten minutes. Some alternatives that keep the laughs and take the pressure off:

- **A trivia card on every table.** Ten questions about the couple, and the table with the most right answers wins a bottle or first place in the dessert line.
- **A guest quiz in the program.** Print five questions about the couple in the wedding program, with the answers revealed in a toast.
- **A live game on the screen.** All the guests answer questions about the couple from their phones, and a leaderboard shows who knows them best.
